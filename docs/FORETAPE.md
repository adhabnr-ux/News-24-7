# Foretape: News247 on your home screen, with instant push alerts

**Foretape** ("before the tape moves") is News247's own phone app, built for the way a trading desk reads news: what happened, which way it cuts, what happened last time, and how far ahead of everyone else you are. It's a website you add to your home screen, so it looks and behaves like an app:

- **Push notifications on your lock screen**, sent the moment News247 decides a story matters.
- The same alert also appears in the open app instantly, over a live stream.
- **No app store, no phone number, no Meta/WhatsApp account, no fees.**

It works on iPhone and iPad (iOS/iPadOS 16.4 or newer), Android, and desktop Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari.

```
News247 server ──(encrypted Web Push)──▶ Apple / Google push service ──▶ your phone's lock screen
      └──────────(live stream)──────────────────────────────────────────▶ the open Foretape app
```

## Install on iPhone (2 minutes)

1. On your phone, open this link in **Safari**. Your server's address and `DASHBOARD_TOKEN` are under Render → news247 → Environment.
   ```
   https://<your-app>.onrender.com/app/?token=<DASHBOARD_TOKEN>
   ```
   Or open the **Setup page** (`/setup?token=…`) on a computer and scan its Foretape QR code with the iPhone camera.
2. Tap **Share** (the square with the arrow), then **Add to Home Screen**, then **Add**.
3. Open **Foretape** from your home screen and tap **Turn on alerts**. When iOS asks, tap **Allow**.
4. A test notification arrives within a second or two: *"🔴 Foretape test: alerts will arrive like this"*.

That's it. Close the app; alerts still arrive.

> **Why the home screen?** Apple delivers web notifications only to sites added to the home screen, and the permission prompt can only come from a tap. That's why there's a button and not an automatic prompt. The app's first screen walks you through it.

**Android / desktop:** open the same link in Chrome, Edge or Firefox, tap **Turn on alerts**, and allow. Installing it (menu → *Install app* / *Add to Home screen*) is optional but gives it its own icon.

## What you see

Foretape opens on a **live photograph**: a rocket climbing out of its own smoke at golden hour. It is not a video or a gradient. A real launch photo is rendered by a WebGL shader (`scene.js`) that adds what a still can't have:

- **Depth.** Tilt your phone (or move the mouse) and the near smoke moves more than the far sky.
- **Life.** Clouds drift and the smoke billows. The plume flickers, heat shimmers above the pad, embers rise, and the wet ground glints. A new alert makes the plume flare.
- **Altitude.** The camera climbs as you scroll: past the cirrus, off the top of the photo and into a starfield. Each tab sits at its own altitude, so switching tabs flies the camera. An altitude rail on the right edge shows where you are.
- **Mood.** The light is a little brighter while the market is open and dusky while it's closed. A CRITICAL alert in the last 15 minutes warms the edges of the frame.
- **Callouts pinned to the rocket** show live data: how many sources are up, the last alert and who it hit, and your median lead over the news. They are projected through the same camera as the shader, so they stay on the rocket.

Everything on top of the photo is glass, lit like the scene: a cold rim on the top edge, a warm bounce on the bottom, a highlight that slides with the tilt of your phone, and film grain. Cards swing up out of depth as they scroll in and curve on a cylinder as they pass eye level. Swipe a detail sheet down to dismiss it.

**Phones that can't take it:** on WebGL-less browsers the plain photo is shown with CSS parallax; with *Reduce Motion* turned on, a still frame; and a slow GPU lowers the render resolution automatically. Add `?scale=0.5` to the address to render fewer pixels yourself.

**iPhone motion permission.** iOS asks before a web page may read the gyroscope. Foretape shows a *"✦ Tilt your phone: enable motion"* chip once, and the Desk tab has a switch. On Android and desktop there's no prompt (mouse movement drives the tilt on desktop).

### Every alert comes with an analysis

| Part | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **The little thing** | For a small or mid cap: the company, its market cap and size band, the catalyst, the typical move **for a company that size** (*"Contract from U.S. Army worth 64% of market cap · +36–96% typical"*) and the deal size as a share of the company. On cards it is the gold **MICRO CAP / SMALL CAP / MID CAP** pill; radar alerts carry **◉ Radar** with relative volume. See [SMALLCAPS.md](SMALLCAPS.md). |
| **The play** | **▲ Bullish / ▼ Bearish / ◆ Two-way**, a 5-bar **conviction** meter (Low → Maximum, from the score), the **direct** tickers and the **read-through** names (suppliers, competitors, the sector basket). When the headline itself doesn't say which way it cuts (a Fed hike, a tariff post, a Polymarket jump), the direction comes from how the most similar past events traded, and the app says so. |
| **Precedents** | The most similar market-moving events on record (150+ since 2016, including every Jun–Oct 2026 mover we traced), matched on the same themes, entities and tickers the scorer found. Each shows what the stocks did then. Example: an FHFA post about VantageScore → *"Last time (Sep 28, 2026): FICO −25 to −27%"*. The top precedent is also on the lock-screen notification. |
| **The tape since** | Each direct ticker's move since the alert ("FICO −9.76% since alert"), live from the price feed. |
| **The edge** | How many seconds after publication Foretape caught it, where it was first seen, and when the mainstream caught up: *"Beat CNBC by 6m 52s"*. It's measured live: when CNBC, Yahoo Finance, Google News, MarketWatch, WSJ, Bloomberg, NYT, Fortune or Axios later carries the same story, the gap is recorded. |
| **The story** | Every headline in the story, first one first: the source, how long after the first, its score, and a link. (With one headline per source: **Who carried it**.) |
| **Earlier alerts like this** | Alerts of the same kind on the same stock in the last 180 days, each with its grade, and how they did by the close as a group. |
| **Why Foretape flagged it** | The full score breakdown: every keyword, theme, authority and source signal that counted. |
| **Was this useful?** | 👍 / 👎, one vote per phone; the counts are every phone's, kept on the alert. |
| **Edge score** | One 0-100 number per alert (also on its card), a weighted composite of the measured facts on its page: how alerts like it did (45%), whether the stock had already moved (20%), how many kinds of evidence agree (15%) and severity (20%). Every part is listed; it summarizes, it doesn't forecast. |
| **Priced in?** | How far the stock had already moved in the 15 minutes before the alert (from the live tape's recent path), and how alerts that came after a move like that did: *fresh* (under 1%), *already moved its way*, or *moved against it first*. The card says it too: *"ACME had already moved +2.5% in the 15 minutes before (its way)"*. |
| **How alerts like this did** | The base rate: every alert is graded (an hour later and at the close, on its own stock, in its own direction), and a new alert carries how its group did over the last 60 days: *"Alerts like this (Options): 62% went their way in 1h, 58% by the close · 31 graded"*. Groups are headlines and macro by severity, then radar, options, insider buys and confluence. Shown only once 8+ alerts in the group are graded; on the card, the sheet and the lock-screen notification. The sheet also draws the **typical path**: the group's median move in the alert's direction at 1 hour, the close, the next day and 5 days. A group that went its way **40% of the time or less over 20+ graded alerts** is flagged **⚠ Historically fades** (and the notification says *historically fades*): the alert may be worth fading, not following. |
| **What lines up** | On a **confluence** alert: each piece of evidence, its side and its weight (see below). |
| **Act on it** | A door to each stock's page, **▲/▼ Track** (a paper call at the live price, kept on your desk with its P&L; the direction follows the play) and **Share**: the phone's share sheet (or a copied link) with a deep link straight to this alert. Whoever opens it needs their own access key. |

### Confluence alerts: when the evidence piles up on one side

The other alerts each see one thing: a headline, options bought, an insider buy, the tape igniting. A confluence alert fires when **independent kinds of evidence line up on the same side of the same stock**: the evidence tally (below) leans clearly one way, with **3 or more kinds** on that side (headlines, options flow, insider buying, the tape), a combined weight of **4+**, and at least one non-insider signal from **the last two hours** (something is happening now, not just an old filing). Weight **7+** is HIGH and pushed (at most 8 a day); under that it's MEDIUM, in the app and pushed to phones watching the stock. Each stock and side fires once a day, only while the market (pre-market through after-hours) is trading. The title says what lined up: *"▲ ACME: insider buying, headlines and options flow line up"*. Like every alert it is graded, so its own base rate builds up.

Your open price alerts and tracked calls on this stock are listed right there. |
| **Recent tape** | Its intraday price line. |

| **On the tape** | An ignition (with its burst volume), the radar's read (dollars traded, relative volume) and the latest options hit (call vs put premium, top contracts). |
| **What options price in** | The at-the-money straddle (call + put at the strike nearest the stock) for the next expiries within 60 days: *"By Fri Oct 23: ±6.1% ($94.20 – $106.40)"*, with 30-day implied vol. The average move, how many beat, and, next to the implied move above, whether options price the next report bigger or smaller than its usual move (*"±7.5% (0.75× its usual move)"*). Cached 12 hours. |
| **Foretape on it** | Every alert on the stock in the last 30 days, its direction, its move **since the alert** (from the alert's own reference price), and its grade an hour later and at the close. |

## Your desk: per-phone watchlist, price alerts, tracked calls

Each phone (each browser) gets a random id the first time it opens Foretape. Everything you set is kept on the server under that id, so the server can respect it when it pushes, and it survives restarts (SQLite, plus the `STATE_DB` Postgres when set).

- **Watchlist** (up to 60 stocks). Alerts on them get the **Mine** filter and the gold ★, and **a MEDIUM alert on a stock you watch reaches your lock screen** (MEDIUM normally only shows in the app), marked ★ in the title. Turn that off with **Watchlist pushes**. Adding a stock also adds it to the live quotes.
- **Price alerts** (up to 40 open): above a price, below a price, or a move of ±N% on the day. Checked every 15 seconds against the freshest live price; when one hits, this phone alone gets a push (*"🔔 NVDA is above $200.00 · $200.40 (+2.10% today)"*), the app shows a toast, and tapping the push opens the stock's page. Each fires once; it then shows ✓ with the price it fired at.
- **Price alerts** can **re-arm every day** (*every day*: after firing, armed again the next session), and come as **trailing**: *falls N% from its high* (the high since you set it, followed on every quote).
- **Tracked calls** (up to 40 open): a paper long or short from an alert or a stock's page, at the live price when you tap. The desk shows each one's P&L live; **Levels** sets a **stop and a target** (a long's stop under the entry and target over it, a short's the other way), each pushed to this phone once when the live price reaches it (*"🎯 ACME hit your target ($110.00) · Long from $104.00, now $110.20 · +5.96%"*); **Close** books it at the live price. Closed calls build your record: how many, how many won, the average. It's how you learn which of your reads work. **The system never trades.**
- **Track record:** every alert Foretape sends is graded on its own, per stock, an hour after it, at its session's close (an after-hours alert is graded at the next session's close), at the next session's close and five sessions on (weekends and market holidays skipped; slow signals like insider buys are judged on their own timescale), in the alert's direction: + means the stock went the alert's way. The desk shows each kind's hit rate and median move (headlines by severity, radar, options, insider buys) over 30 days. Alerts without a direction count toward neither side.
- **First run:** a new phone with an empty desk opens **Make Foretape yours**: tap the stocks you follow (24 widely followed names, or type others), the words that matter to you (*tariff, FDA, buyback, guidance…*), and how much to push (*Only the big ones* = CRITICAL only on this phone, *Normal*, or *Everything, in a 15-minute digest*). **Set up from scratch** on the Desk opens it again.
- **This week** (and **your week**, pushed Fridays at 16:30 ET): calls closed this week with the record and the best one, open calls, how the alerts on your stocks did by the close, and next week: which of your stocks report and the high-impact dates.
- **Track record by first source:** which first-to-publish sources' alerts went their way (an hour later and at the close), for every source with 3+ graded headline alerts.
- **Watchlist:** sort *as added*, *by move* or *A-Z*; each row draws its last half hour as a sparkline.
- **Undo:** removing a watched stock, a holding, a price alert or a smart alert shows **Undo** for six seconds.
- **Shortcuts & links:** every page's link (a stock, an alert, a search, the Brief, the Desk, the calendar feed) with Copy, for iOS Shortcuts (*Open URL*), widgets and bookmarks.
- **Chime on alerts:** a short two-note chime for HIGH, CRITICAL or your stocks while the app is open.
- **Heatmap:** your watched stocks as tiles colored by today's move, above the watchlist.
- **Saved:** ☆ Save on any alert keeps it on the Desk with its grades.
- **Snooze:** quiet a category (headlines, radar, options, insider, macro, confluence) on this phone for an hour; tap again to end it. **Skip what fades:** don't push alert kinds whose base rate says they fade (40% or less over 20+ graded); CRITICAL still comes.
- **Auto-why:** at 11:00 and 14:30 ET, a watched stock moving 4%+ with no headline behind it gets the EDGAR + news hunt (once a day per stock), so its page, the report card and the tape can say why.
- **Display (this phone):** *Cinematic* (the 3D launch scene and glass) or *Clear*: no 3D scene, no blur, solid panels, no reveal animations (legible in sunlight, light on older phones' batteries); *Larger text*; *Light* (a light theme, clear-mode based); *Compact* (more rows per screen: cards drop their secondary lines); *Vibrate on alerts* (Android: a short buzz for HIGH, CRITICAL or your stocks while the app is open); *One notification per stock* (a newer alert on a stock replaces the older one on the lock screen).
- **Keyboard and screen readers:** every tappable row is focusable and opens with Enter or Space; toasts are announced.
- **A move alert for every stock you add:** off, or ±3/5/8/10% on the day, created automatically when you watch a stock.
- **Also send to:** this phone's alerts copied to a **Discord** webhook, a **Telegram** bot chat or an **ntfy** topic: everything that reaches this phone (alerts, watchlist pushes, keyword hits, price alerts, reminders, digests), as text. The server only ever posts to those three services' own hosts; the URL or token is shown masked.
- **Notification buttons:** on Android and desktop (iOS doesn't show web-push buttons), an alert's notification has **Mute SYM 4h** next to *Open source*: it mutes the stock on that phone without opening the app.
- **Track record by time of day and "priced in":** the record also splits every graded alert by when it went out (pre-market, the open, midday, power hours, after hours) and by how far the stock had moved before it.
- **Your data:** **Backup (JSON)** downloads the whole desk; **Calls (CSV)** downloads your open and closed calls with entries, exits, P&L, levels, tags, notes and lessons.
- **Groups:** tap ⋯ on a watched stock to put it in a group (*Earnings plays*, *Semis*); the watchlist shows its groups.
- **Your taste:** what this phone finds useful, per alert kind, from what you do: 👍/👎, saves, tracked calls, opens. *Best first* on the tape ranks your favorite kinds higher; a kind you keep voting down (3+ downvotes, twice your positives) offers to turn itself off.
- **Follow-ups:** an hour after an alert on a stock you watch or hold, if the stock moved 2%+ since (from the alert's reference price), a push says how it played out (*"↪ ACME +3.1% since the alert an hour ago"*). Off until you turn it on.

The Desk's **Uptime and the record** shows how much of each horizon is graded and how much came from the bars, with **Fill the gaps from daily bars now**. `POST /api/bars/fill`.
- **The uptime ledger:** the desk notes a heartbeat every minute; on start, the gap since the last one is measured, and the part that fell in the regular session is kept as an outage (also in the durable state store when `STATE_DB` is set, so a host that wipes its disk on restart still knows how long it was gone). Slept through 10+ minutes of a session? Every phone gets a push a few minutes after waking: *"😴 Foretape was asleep during the session · Asleep Wed 10:00–11:30 ET: 90 min of the session went unread. Moving now with no alert: BETA -6.0%"*. The Desk shows the share of market hours awake over 20 sessions and each outage, and **What we caught, what we missed** marks a miss whose move fell while the desk was asleep as *asleep*, so a sleep isn't mistaken for a blind spot. The fix for sleeping is outside the app (a paid instance, or `general.keepalive_url` with an uptime pinger on a free one); this makes the cost visible. `GET /api/uptime`.
- **The desk's advice:** many panels each have a verdict on a setting (tune my pushes finds a better edge bar, what your settings cost finds one holding back winners, the alert line's report finds the line too high, the call's replay shows whether following it pays), each read in its own corner. The Desk's **Advice** reads them all for this phone and lists the changes worth making, most evidence first, each with its numbers and one tap that makes it: switch off a kind of news that keeps losing here (30+ graded by the close at 40% or less with a negative median), switch back on a muted kind whose record recovered (55%+ on 20+), push with the tuned setting (when it beats yours by 0.2%+ a push), fix a setting that's costing you, lower the alert line, and follow the call on paper (when "act now" went its way 60%+ on 30+ replayed alerts and no autopilot follows it yet). **Not now** hides an item for good. Evidence, not orders: nothing changes until tapped. `GET /api/advice` (per phone).
- **Sympathy, learned:** sympathy pushes pick a news stock's peers by industry, but whether a given peer actually follows that stock's news was never measured, because only the alert's own stock was priced. Now every HIGH alert with a direction notes up to eight peers' live prices when it goes out (`edge.peer_refs`), and their moves an hour later and at the close (`edge.peer_out`). Pooled per pair over 180 days, each pair gets a record: *"When ACME has news, BETA followed its way by the close 83% of the time here (median +1.5%, 6)."* A sympathy push carries its pair's line once the pair has 5+ graded days, and each stock page lists **who follows its news** (and who goes the other way). History, not a forecast, and the counts are always shown. `GET /api/followers/{SYM}`.
- **The wait is over:** when the call says wait (for the open, or for a pullback), a push now says when the wait ends. From 9:35 to 10:00 ET, each wait-for-the-open alert from before the bell gets one push. It says where the stock opened against the alert price and where it is now. It also gives how news like it went on after the open from a gap that size (into the open's record), with a read: *the record from here favors acting*, *still says no rush*, or *is mixed*. During the alert's session, each wait-for-a-pullback alert gets one push when the price comes back to its patient entry (the winners' median dip, now stamped on the alert with its plan), with the odds once filled there (losers dip too). Pushes go to phones the alert reached and phones that watch, hold or have a call on the stock (Settings → *The wait is over*). Each ended wait is stamped on its alert (`edge.wait`), and the sheet says when and where. **Did waiting pay?** Every wait is graded against acting at the alert price by the close, its way. The open means entering at the first print. The pullback means a limit at the patient entry: filled when the alert's worst dip reached it, and earning nothing (against the alert's move) when it didn't. Each kind of wait gets the share of times it beat acting, what it added on average, and how many never came back to the level. It is shown on the call's record (the Desk) and in the push (8+ graded). If waiting doesn't pay, the call's wait rule is wrong and it shows there.
- **Fill the intraday record from minute bars:** the daily bars fill missed closes; the minutes a sleeping server missed are on file too. Twice an hour, alerts 4 hours to 25 days old whose session closed half an hour ago are read for the intraday stamps they lack. Those are the news clock (5, 15, 30 minutes), the hour, the first print after the bell for news from outside the session, heat (best run and worst dip before the close), the market's hour and each peer's hour and close. Each comes from one-minute bars on the live stamp's clock (a bar ending a minute before to 2.5 minutes after its moment), from Alpaca when the server has its keys, else Yahoo's one-minute chart with pre- and post-market. Filled stamps are listed on their row (`filled`; heat is marked `exc.filled`), and an alert whose bars can't be had is tried twice, then left alone. The news clock, live odds, heat and patient entries, into the open, the call's clock fact and sympathy learned all stop losing the alerts a sleep swallowed. **Uptime and the record** now shows the intraday horizons beside the closes, with **Fill the gaps from bars now** (both kinds of bars).
- **The alert line, per kind of news:** the line test grades headlines just under the line beside the alerts just over it, but a score means different things for different news. Pooled by kind of news, the same test now says where each kind's line belongs:
  - **Lower it 5** where a kind's headlines just under its line (0-5 points) went their way by the close as often as its alerts just over it (within 3 points), with a positive median, on 15+ graded each side.
  - **Raise it 5** where its alerts just over the line went their way 40% or less with a negative median (15+).

  A kind's line moves 5 points at a time, at most 10 from the main line and never up to HIGH. It moves in one tap from the Desk's **The alert line, tested** panel or from the advice, logged with the main line's moves and reversible. Headlines are read against their kind's line when they arrive: a lowered kind's near misses become MEDIUM alerts, and a raised kind's become shadows, still graded. `POST /api/line/kind` (`{"kind", "offset": -5, "why"}`, `offset: null` to reset), and `GET /api/line` lists every kind as `kinds`.
- **Float and short interest, graded:** the same contract win means one thing for a stock with 8 million shares in its float and another for one with 800 million, and a crowded short has to buy back. Every news alert's stock now carries its float and short interest (the share of the float sold short), read from Yahoo's key statistics and kept a week per stock. A stock not read yet is read in the background and its alert stamped when the numbers arrive; when they can't be read, nothing is stamped. Every alert is graded by the close by float band (under 10M, 10-50M, 50-200M, 200M+ shares) and by short band (under 5%, 5-15%, 15-30%, 30%+), for every kind of news and each kind, with the winners' median best run. An alert carries its band's record (*"🧮 Float 8.2M · 22% short: contract win on a float under 10M shares went its way 71% by the close (median +6.2%, winners ran +9.0%, 18)"*). A strong (60%+) or weak (40%-) record counts in the call as its own fact, graded in the call's replay like the rest. The Watch tab has the board. `GET /api/float`.
- **Dilution risk, from the filings:** a small company whose stock runs on news can sell shares into the run, from a shelf registration with prospectus supplements, often within days. That ends many small-cap runs. Every news alert's stock now carries its state from the company's own SEC filings (EDGAR's submissions API, read a day at a time with the server's named user agent):
  - **selling shares now**: a 424B prospectus in the last 180 days;
  - **a shelf on file**: an S-3 or F-3 in the last three years with no recent sale;
  - **no shelf**.

  A stock not read yet is read in the background (when the SEC source is on) and its alert stamped when the filings arrive; when they can't be read, nothing is stamped. Graded by the close and five sessions on, its way, for every kind of news and each kind. An alert carries its state's record on its sheet (*"📄 Selling shares: 2 prospectuses (424B) in 6 months… contract win on a stock with selling shares now: 33% its way by the close (9), 0% five sessions on"*), and a weak (40%-) or strong (60%+) one counts in the call. The Watch tab has the board. `GET /api/dilution`.
- **Your changes, graded:** the advice, the line test and the line per kind each recommend a change, and a tap makes it. Now every change is checked against what came after it, by the close:
  - **A kind switched off from the advice** (tracked per phone): its alerts since. It holds at 45% or less; it backfired at 55%+ on 10+, since winners are being kept off the phone.
  - **A lowered line** (main or per kind): the alerts that only exist because of the move. It holds while they do as well as the alerts over the old line, within 5 points.
  - **A raised line**: the headlines it kept in the shadow. It backfired if they went their way 55%+.

  A change that backfired gets a one-tap undo on the Desk's **Your changes, graded** and in the advice. Only the line as it stands can be put back. `GET /api/changes` (per phone).

### The morning Brief

## Keeping phones subscribed on Render's free plan

Render's free plan wipes the disk on every restart. Foretape handles this in two layers:

1. **The signing key never changes.** It's derived from your `DASHBOARD_TOKEN`, so your phone's subscription stays valid across restarts and redeploys.
2. **The subscription list** lives on the server's disk. After a restart it's empty until your phone reopens Foretape, which re-registers automatically and silently. The Control tab warns you when this applies.

**For zero gaps, add a free Postgres** (for example [Neon](https://neon.tech): free, no card). In Render → news247 → Environment, set:

```
STATE_DB = postgres://user:password@host/dbname?sslmode=require
```

News247 then keeps a copy of the subscriptions there and restores them on start. It only writes when a phone subscribes or unsubscribes, so free databases that sleep when idle are fine. If you already set `WHATSAPP_STATE_DB` for the WhatsApp pairing backup, Foretape uses that one automatically.

## Privacy and security

- **The mainstream catch-up, graded:** each alert already recorded when CNBC, Reuters, Bloomberg, the WSJ and the like carried the same story (the lead), but not what the head start was worth. Now the stock's price is noted at that moment, so the day splits in two, its way: before the catch-up (what the head start captured) and after it (whether the story went further once everyone had it, by the close). Pooled by kind of news and head start (under 15 minutes, 15-60, 1-4 hours, 4 hours+), the record gives the share of the winners' day made before the catch-up and how often it went further after. When the mainstream catches up on a story on a phone's stock (or one it was pushed), that phone gets one push: *"📰 CNBC has ACME now, 20 min after your alert · Since the alert: +2.5% its way. Contract win the mainstream caught up on in 15-60 minutes: 67% of the winners' day came before it; after it, 41% went further by the close"* (Settings → *Mainstream caught up*). The sheet shows where the stock stood at the catch-up, and the Watch tab has the board. `GET /api/catchup`.
- **Halts, graded:** every halt from Nasdaq's feed now notes its stock's price and the day's move into it (the way it was going), and from its resumption the stock is graded 5 and 30 minutes later and at the close against the halt price. Pooled by halt code and direction (*"Volatility Trading Pause (LULD) on the way up kept going: 5 minutes after the resumption 58%, 30 minutes after 51%, by the close 44%"*), the record rides on the resumption push to watchers (8+ graded), and the Watch tab has the board. Halts with no live price or no direction into them are left out. `GET /api/halts/record`.
- **The second leg:** news moves rarely go in one line: the first leg comes with the headline, then the stock rests, and the ones that matter often break out again later. The desk now watches today's alerts for that breakout. A second leg is when the stock trades at a new day's extreme its way, an hour or more after the alert, 2%+ from the alert price, after 30+ minutes without a new extreme (a real rest, not one long climb). It's stamped on its alert (`edge.leg2`, shown on the sheet), and phones that got the alert, or watch, hold or have a call on the stock, get one push: *"🚀 ACME second leg: a new high 1.7 h after its news · A new day's high after 80 minutes without one: +4.6% its way since the alert. Second legs went on by the close 57% (median +1.1%, 23)"* (Settings → *Second legs*). Graded from the breakout to the close by kind of news, with a Watch-tab board. `GET /api/legs`.
- **Market-wide news, graded by theme:** a headline about tariffs, the Fed, an oil shock, a war or a bond-yield threshold names no stock, so it was graded on nothing, though it moves whole sectors. An alert carrying a market-wide theme now notes a basket of ETFs when it goes out (SPY, QQQ, IWM, the sector SPDRs, semis, regional banks, Treasuries, gold, oil), graded an hour later and at the close. Per theme, each ETF's record gives how often it rose, its median move, and the same against SPY. A theme's biggest movers ride on its next headline's sheet (*"🌐 Trade-war headlines here (9), in the hour: SMH −1.1% · XLU +0.2%"*), and the Watch tab lists every theme. Raw moves, not "its way": a market-wide headline rarely has a clean direction. `GET /api/themes`.
- **Volume confirmation, graded:** a headline is a claim, and volume is the market voting on it. Every alert now notes its stock's relative volume (today's volume against its normal for this time of day, from the tape) when it goes out and again 15 minutes in (`edge.rvol`). Graded by the close by band 15 minutes in (under 2x, 2-5x, 5-10x, 10x+), for every kind of news and each kind. The first read's band record counts in the call. At 15 minutes, when the alert's band has a clear record (60%+ or 40%-, 8+ graded), phones with the stock get one push: *"📊 ACME 15 minutes in: confirmed by volume · 7.5x its normal volume 15 minutes in. Contract win on 5-10x normal volume 15 minutes in: went its way 71% by the close"*, or *"🔇 … no volume behind it"* (Settings → *Volume confirmation*). The sheet shows both reads, and the Watch tab has the board. `GET /api/rvol`.
- **Your book on market-wide news:** a phone with a portfolio holds stocks, not ETFs. Each holding is read through its industry's ETF (semis via SMH, software via IGV, biotech via XBI, regional banks via KRE), else its sector's SPDR, else SPY. When a market-wide headline goes out, the theme's record for those ETFs gives the book's expected hour in dollars and as a share of the book. Holders get one push when it's 0.3%+ of the book: *"🌐 Trade-war headline: your book −1.9% in the hour, by the record · Your book moved −$203 (−1.9%) in the hour after trade-war headlines like it (9), by its sectors' records: SMH −2.0% for ACME; SPY −0.6% for ZZZZ"* (Settings → *Your book on market-wide news*). The headline's sheet shows the same for this phone. It's an estimate from sector records, not the stocks themselves. `GET /api/book-theme/{alert id}` (per phone).
- **The odds learn the new records:** the calibrated odds model now also reads, as of when each alert went out:
  - its float and short interest;
  - its dilution state;
  - its volume at the alert;
  - how late it was to its story;
  - its stock's personality;
  - whether it's a macro day;
  - whether its kind is running hot or cold.

  Those facts are now stamped before the odds and the plan are computed, so both see them. The model still has to beat the plain hit rate on the newest quarter it never saw before its odds are used. **What each record adds**: once a day the same held-out test is rerun without each record's inputs. The skill lost is what that record adds to the odds (*"Float and short interest +3.1 pts, on 62% of the test alerts"*); near zero or below means the odds don't need it yet. It's shown on the Desk's odds panel.
- **The call, live:** the call is made when an alert goes out, but the day keeps grading facts on it: its volume 15 minutes in, the mainstream catching up (and what's usually left after), a second leg. Each has its own record. The call now re-reads itself every minute with those facts for the day's alerts. When the verdict changes (*watch → act now*, *act now → skip*), it's stamped on the alert (`edge.call_now`, with the price and the fact that moved it) and the sheet shows it. Phones tracking a call on the stock or that got the alert get one push: *"▶ ACME: the call is now act now (was watch)"* (Settings → *The call, live*). Every turn is graded from its price to the close, and the call's record on the Desk shows how calls that turned to act or skip did.
- **Into the event:** stocks (small biotechs above all) often rise into a known binary event, then the event decides. Every FDA decision, readout and panel the calendar captured is graded two days after from its stock's daily closes (Alpaca's or Yahoo's daily bars):
  - **the run-up:** the ten sessions before;
  - **the event day;**
  - **what holding through added** over selling the session before.

  Pooled by kind of event and company size (*"Into FDA decisions, small caps: rose 64% of the time in the 10 sessions before (median +8.1%); holding through the event added −3.2%"*). Ten sessions before an event on a phone's stock, between 9:35 and 10:30 ET, that phone gets one push with the record (Settings → *Into the event*). The Calendar tab opens with the board. Events whose bars can't be read are left out. `GET /api/runups`.
- **Paper autopilots for the live signals:** an autopilot rule can now follow a signal that arrives during the day:
  - **second legs:** in at the breakout, at the live price, with a fresh plan;
  - **the call turning to act:** in at the turn.

  Each gets the usual stop, target, exit and three-a-day cap, and its own forward-tested record and equity curve. A tap from the second-legs board or the call's record turns one on.
- **Which signals work here:** beyond the alerts, the desk pushes a dozen kinds of live signal, each graded in its own corner:
  - the wait is over;
  - the mainstream caught up;
  - second legs;
  - volume 15 minutes in;
  - the call turning;
  - run-ups into events;
  - sympathy flags.

  The Desk's **Which signals work here** reads them all for this phone and ranks them by their forward record, each with its numbers and the push switch that controls it. A signal is strong at 60%+ with a positive median (8+ graded) and weak at 45% or less or a negative median. The advice offers to turn off a weak signal this phone still gets, and back on a strong one it switched off. `GET /api/signals` (per phone).
- **A budget for signal pushes:** on a busy morning the live signals can stack up, so each phone gets an hourly budget for them (Settings → *Signal pushes an hour*: 4, 8 or 12, or no limit; 8 by default). A signal over the budget isn't lost: it's folded, and a digest push every half hour lists what was folded (*"🗂 2 more signals while your budget was full · 🚀 ACME second leg · 🚀 BETA second leg"*), each still on its alert's sheet. Alerts and price alerts are never folded here; they have their own controls.
- **How to enter each kind of news:** the desk knows six ways into a news move, each graded in its own corner. Now they're graded side by side on the same alerts, each from its own entry to the close:
  - at the alert;
  - at the open (news before the bell);
  - at the patient entry (when the alert's dip reached it);
  - on the second leg;
  - on volume confirmation (5x+ normal volume 15 minutes in);
  - when the call turned to act.

  The conditional ways only enter some alerts, so each shows how many of its kind's alerts it entered. Per kind of news, the best is the one with the best average from its entry (8+ entries): *"🎯 How to enter contract win news here: on the second leg (+1.3% on average from the entry, 70% its way, 12 of 30); at the alert +0.7%"*. It's on every alert's sheet, a section of each playbook (and one of its rules of thumb), and a Watch-tab board. `GET /api/entries`.
- **The tape today:** some days the news works, and some days nothing does. Each past session's first 8 directional news alerts are read an hour in: **cold** when 3 or fewer went their way, **hot** at 6 or more. That day's remaining alerts are graded by the close (*"on cold days here, the rest of the day's alerts went their way 38% by the close (median −0.6%, 14 sessions) vs 56% on normal days"*). Live, once today's first 8 have their hour, the tape is read. On a clearly cold or hot day (the state's record 10+ points from normal on 5+ sessions each), every phone gets one push (Settings → *The tape today*). The Watch tab shows today's read beside the record. `GET /api/tape`.
- **How to exit each kind of news:** the counterpart to the entries. The same alerts are exited five ways from the alert price, its way:
  - an hour in;
  - at the close;
  - the next day;
  - five sessions on;
  - the plan's bracket: its stop or target, whichever the stock reached first; the stop when both were reached the same day (the conservative read); else the close.

  Per kind of news the best exit is the one with the best average (8+ graded): *"🚪 Exit contract win news here: the next day (+1.1% on average, 70% its way); at the close +0.2%"*. It's on every alert's sheet, each playbook (with a rule of thumb) and a Watch-tab board. Each kind now has a way in and a way out from its own record. `GET /api/exits-kind`.
- **Your reaction time, priced (and Track from the lock screen):** every paper call opened from an alert records when it was opened, and the alert records when it went out and its price. So each call shows how long its phone took to act and what that cost: the entry against the alert price, its way.
  - The Desk's **Your reaction time** gives the median delay and cost, the cost by speed (under 5 minutes, 5-15, 15-60, an hour+), and the cost per kind of news beside how fast that news plays out (the news clock).
  - The fix is a tap: alert pushes with a plan now carry a **Track (paper)** button where the phone shows notification buttons (Android, desktop). It opens the paper call with the alert's plan (stop, target, size) straight from the lock screen and confirms it with a notification.
  - Calls opened that way are marked, and the panel compares their cost to calls opened in the app.

  `GET /api/reaction` (per phone), `POST /api/track-alert` (`{"alert_id"}`, per phone).
- **The playbook autopilot:** with each kind of news' best way in and best way out graded, a paper autopilot can now follow the playbook itself. Each alert is entered the way its kind enters best here:
  - at the alert;
  - at the session's first print;
  - at its patient entry, if the dip comes that day;
  - on its second leg;
  - on volume confirmation (5x+ normal volume 15 minutes in);
  - when the call turns to act.

It's a forward test of the whole learning system, with its own record and equity curve; *"🤖 Follow the playbook on paper"* on the entries board turns it on.

- **Where to put the stop for each kind of news:** the piece between the entries and the exits. Each graded alert kept its worst dip, its way, until its session's close, so any stop can be replayed on it from the alert price:
  - no stop (out at the close);
  - 1%, 2%, 3% and 5%;
  - the record's stop: just past where 80% of its kind's winners bottomed (the playbook's heat rule);
  - the plan's own stop.

  A stop that was reached fills at its price, before any gain that day (the conservative read). Per kind of news, the best stop is the one with the best average (8+ graded). Two numbers price the trade-off each stop makes: how often it **cut** the call, and how many of those it **shook out** (the stock finished the day its way anyway). For example: *"🛑 Stop contract win news here: 2.0% from the entry (+0.7% on average, 12); no stop -0.7%; a 1% stop shook out 67% of the calls it cut"*.

The playbook autopilot now uses it: a call on a time exit carries its kind's stop, while the plan's bracket keeps the plan's stop and target. The 9:20 game plan names it too (*"in at the open, out the next day, stop 3.0%"*). `GET /api/stops-kind`.
- **What the record learned this week:** the records re-fit every hour, so a playbook can change without anyone noticing. Each day the desk now keeps a snapshot of its verdicts:
  - per kind of news: its best way in, way out and stop, and how often it went its way by the close;
  - per signal: its grade.

  The Watch tab's **What the record learned this week** compares today's verdicts with the snapshot from a week ago:

  - signals that changed grade.

  Every Friday a push says it in four lines. Settings → *What the record learned, Friday* turns it off. `GET /api/learned`.
- **Picks checked out of sample:** each best way in, way out and stop is the best of several averages on as few as eight alerts, and the best of several noisy averages is high partly by luck. So every pick is now checked the way a trading system should be: choose on the past, grade on what came after. Per kind of news, the alerts are split by time:
  - the older two-thirds pick a style (the best average there, 5+);
  - the newest third grades it against the default (at the alert, at the close, no stop; 4+ each).

  A pick has three verdicts:
  - **Held:** the older alerts picked the same style, and it did at least as well as the default on the newest third.
  - **Didn't hold:** the older alerts picked something else (not stable), or it trailed the default on the newest third. The desk then acts on the default for that kind: the sheet, the 9:20 game plan, the playbook autopilot and the weekly *what the record learned*. It says why: *"🚪 Exit contract win news here: at the close … · ✗ on the newest third it made -0.5% vs +0.6% at the close (4): didn't hold, so at the close"*.
  - **Unverified:** too few new alerts to tell yet; the pick stands, marked "…".

- **The playbook's promises against its results:** the paper autopilots are the forward test of everything the desk learns, since they act on alerts nobody had seen when the records were fit. Each autopilot's closed calls are now graded against **the plain way on the same alerts**: in at the alert price, out at that session's close, in the call's direction. The difference is what the rule's choices (when to get in and out, where to stop) added or cost, alert for alert, so a good or bad week for the market cancels out. With 10+ graded calls a rule gets a verdict: its choices **add** (+0.25% a call or more), **cost** (-0.25% or worse), or come out **about even**.

  The playbook autopilot also records what the record **promised** when it opened each call: its kind's chosen exit average, or its entry's average when it didn't enter at the alert. So *"the record promised +1.00%, live +0.75%"* says whether the picks keep their word out of sample, kind by kind. On each autopilot's card on the Desk; `GET /api/autopilots/forward` (per phone).
- **The whole playbook, backtested together:** the best way in and the best way out were picked one at a time, but they aren't independent: a stock bought on its second leg has already run, and holding it into tomorrow may be a different trade from holding one bought at the alert. So every pair is replayed on each graded alert, from its own entry price to its own exit:
  - in at the alert: out an hour in, at the close, the next day, five sessions on, or at the plan's bracket;
  - in at the open, at the patient entry, on the second leg or when the call turns: out at the close, the next day or five sessions on (none of them can be sold an hour after the alert);
  - in on volume confirmation (15 minutes in): out an hour in, at the close, the next day or five sessions on.

  Per kind of news the best pair is the one with the best average (8+ alerts had it), checked out of sample against the plain way (in at the alert, out at the close). For example: *"🧭 The playbook for contract win news here, in and out together: at the alert, out the next day (+2.0% on average, 75% its way, 12); the plain way +1.0%"*.

  The playbook autopilot now trades the pair (its kind's separate picks only when no pair is graded yet). Its promise is the pair's average, exactly the backtest of what it does. On the alert's sheet, the kind's playbook, the 9:20 game plan and the Watch tab's **The whole playbook, together** board, with each kind's top three pairs. `GET /api/combos`.
- **The second headline graded:** a stock that just moved on a headline often gets another within days: a follow-up contract, a second analyst, the 8-K behind the press release. Each alert now knows whether it's a **repeat**: the same stock had a directional alert in the 7 days before it, more than 30 minutes before (one story from several outlets counts once). Per kind of news the record compares:
  - first headlines against repeats (how often each went its way by the close, and the average);
  - repeats of the same kind against another kind;
  - repeats after a first headline that worked against one that didn't.

  The alert's sheet flags it: *"🔁 The 2nd headline on ACME in 2 days. Repeat contract win headlines went their way 38% here (21); first ones 61% (30)"*, with the headline before it. So does the 9:20 game plan. The odds model takes it as an input (*Repeat headlines* in what each record adds). The playbook carries a rule for it, and the Watch tab has **The second headline** board. `GET /api/repeats`.
- **The playbook today:** everything the desk learned about trading each kind of news, on one live board (Watch tab, first). Every directional alert since yesterday's close is listed, plus older ones the playbook still holds into the next day or the week. Each shows its kind's pair (way in and way out, picked together and checked out of sample) and where it stands, from the facts the desk records live:
  - **waiting:** for the open, its patient limit, a second leg, volume 15 minutes in, or the call to turn;
  - **skipped:** the entry never came (no second leg by the close, volume didn't confirm, the limit never filled);
  - **in:** entered at its way in's price and marked to the live price;
  - **out:** exited its way (an hour in, at the close, the next day, five sessions on, or the bracket's stop or target), with the result.

  Beside each is the plain way on the same alert (in at the alert, out at the close). The header tallies what's waiting, in and out, the average result, and what the playbook added against the plain way today. Paper only: nothing is traded. `GET /api/playbook-today`.
- **You vs the playbook:** your reaction time priced your delay, and the coach reads your habits. Neither said what you'd have made doing it the record's way. Each paper call you opened from an alert (not the autopilots') is now set beside, on the same alert:
  - the playbook: its kind's pair as stamped when the alert went out, replayed from the alert's graded prices (a pass, 0%, when its entry never came);
  - the plain way.

  With 10+ calls the Desk panel gives a verdict: *"Following the playbook on your 10 calls would have made +2.50% a call more (+3.00% against your +0.50%)"*, or that you beat it, or that it's even. It also breaks the gap down per kind of news and lists the calls with the widest gaps, where the habit behind it shows. `GET /api/you-vs-playbook` (per phone).
- **The playbook on the lock screen:** the playbook's two moments now reach the phones that care about the stock (the alert reached it, or it watches, holds or has a call on the stock), once each:
  - **its way in**, when it comes, for any way in but the alert itself (that one is the alert's own push): *"🧭 ACME: the playbook's way in, on the second leg at $102.00"*, with its way out and what its pair made on average here;
  - **its way out**, with the paper result beside the plain way: *"🏁 ACME: out the next day, +2.0% on the playbook (the plain way +2.0%)"*. There's none for a plain pair (in at the alert, out at the close), whose exit is just the bell.

  Each counts against the phone's hourly budget for signal pushes (over it, they fold into the half-hourly digest). Settings → *The playbook's moves* switches them off. Tapping one opens the alert.
- **The desk acts on the playbook's record:** the desk's advice reads the two playbook verdicts and turns each into one tap:
  - **Follow the playbook on paper,** when it beat your own calls by 0.5% a call or more on the same alerts (10+) and no autopilot follows it yet.
  - **Pause an autopilot** whose choices cost against the plain way on the same alerts over 20+ graded calls.
- **Fixed: boards sharing an id.** *The playbook today* (run 64) reused the price-target board's id, and *Options against the news* (run 45) the unusual-volume board's, so one board could write into the other's place or stay hidden. Both have their own ids now, and a test fails the build if two elements in the app share an id, or if the script looks one up that doesn't exist.
- **The playbook passes:** the best pair for a kind of news is the best of what's there, even when everything lost. Now when no way in and out made money on a kind here, the playbook **passes**: the best pair's average is 0% or less, or its pick fell back to a plain way that loses. Then:
  - the playbook autopilot sits it out;
  - the playbook today shows it as *🚫 pass* with the best pair's average;
  - you vs the playbook counts it as 0%, since the playbook stood aside.
- **Holds that gave back their run:** the playbook holds some kinds of news overnight because on average they keep going. But a stock that ran hard and gave most of it back by the bell isn't the same trade as one that closed near its best. Each graded day is sorted by how much of its best run (its way, from the alert price) it kept at the close:
  - **kept it:** two-thirds or more;
  - **gave back half:** a third to two-thirds;
  - **gave back most:** under a third, or closed against the call.

  Days without a 0.5% run aren't sorted. The record says how each kind of day went overnight, per kind of news (close to the next close, its way). When the playbook's way out is the next day or five sessions on and today's kind of day lost overnight on average (8+ graded):
  - the playbook today board says so before the bell (*"🌙✗ It gave back most of its run today; days like it went -1.5% overnight on average here (0% its way, 10): out at the close instead."*);
  - the playbook autopilot closes such calls at 15:50 ET with that reason;
  - the kind's playbook lists all three kinds of day.
- **The stall rule:** a call that's gone nowhere half an hour after its news ties up money and attention. Every alert's position 30 minutes in is recorded, so each is sorted, its way:
  - **working:** 0.3%+ its way;
  - **stalled:** within 0.3% either side;
  - **against:** 0.3%+ against it.

  Per kind of news, the record says what each did the rest of the day (from the 30-minute price to the close). When stalled ones lost on average (8+ graded), the stall rule is a time stop:
  - the playbook autopilot closes a call it entered at the alert that's still stalled 30 minutes in, once, with the reason;
  - the playbook today board flags calls 30-60 minutes in (*"⏸✗ Stalled 30 minutes in; alerts like it went -0.6% the rest of the day here (0% its way, 10): the time stop says out."*);
  - the kind's playbook lists all three.
- **Crowded days:** when several stocks in one sector get news the same day pointing the same way, each alert looks like its own trade. They're one bet, and the later ones may already be priced in. Each alert now knows how many directional alerts on stocks in its sector, the same way, came before it that day: the first, the second, or the third or later. The record grades each spot by the close, overall and in its sector when that has the counts (8+). Then:
  - the alert's sheet flags the 2nd and later: *"👥 The 3rd technology alert today, the same way (after ACME, BETA). The third or later went their way 44% here (31); the first 61% (120)."*;
  - the odds model takes it as an input (*Crowded days*);
  - the playbook autopilot won't open a 3rd paper call on the same sector, the same way, on the same phone, while two are open: one bet, not three.
- **Denials and walk-backs:** the most dangerous headline for a news trade is the one after it: *"Acme denies report of takeover talks"*, *"Beta clarifies: no plans to raise guidance"*, *"the deal is called off"*. A headline that denies or walks back the news on the same stock, within 3 days of a directional alert, is now linked to the alert it undercuts. The words are a published list: denies, refutes, not in talks, no plans, clarifies, retracts, walks back, calls off, terminates, withdraws, scraps, false report, rumors untrue. Then:
  - **graded:** what stocks did after a denial here, from the denial's price to that session's close, against the original call, and how much of the original move was left at the median;
  - **flagged:** the denial's sheet names the alert it undercuts (*"⚠ A denial 2 hours after its alert ("Acme in takeover talks"). After denials here, stocks moved +4.0% further against the original call by the close (6)…"*); that alert's sheet and its row on the playbook today say it was denied;
  - **pushed:** *"⚠ ACME: a denial after its alert"* goes to the phones the original alert reached or that watch, hold or track the stock (Settings → *Denials*);
  - **acted on:** the playbook autopilot closes its open paper calls on the denied alert at once.
- **Confidence ranges on the playbook:** "+1.2% on average" on 9 alerts and on 90 alerts isn't the same claim. Each kind's best pair and the plain way now carry an 80% range for their average, from a seeded bootstrap (the same record always gives the same range). A best pair whose range still covers the plain way's average is marked **unproven**: it may be the better way, but the record can't yet tell it from luck. That shows on the sheet (*"…; likely +1.3% to +2.7%"*), the board and the kind's playbook rule.
- **The playbook 5 minutes late:** paper fills at the alert price are instant; a person's aren't. A kind's best pair that enters at the alert is now also graded from where the stock stood 5 minutes after the alert (the news clock's m5 checkpoint) to the same exit. When its edge is gone by then (positive at the alert, 0% or less 5 minutes late), the kind is marked **too fast to trade by hand**: the paper autopilot can take it, but a person reading the push mostly can't. It shows on the alert's sheet (*"· 5 minutes late -0.3% (12): too fast to trade by hand"*), the pairs board and the kind's playbook rule.
- **The playbook's day, written up:** the playbook makes many small decisions a day, and on the live board they don't add up to a picture. After the close the desk writes the day up: *"The playbook's day: 6 directional alerts. It took 4 (2 at the alert, 2 on second legs), passed on 1 (where the record says sit out) and 1 entry never came. Cut early: 1 by the time stop, 1 at the close instead of overnight. On paper it made +0.6% a call against +0.6% the plain way on the same alerts (3). Best ACME +3.1%, worst BETA -1.2%."* It tops the playbook today board. A push carries it at 16:10 ET on market days, once (Settings → *The playbook's day*), and tapping it opens the board.
- **The playbook for every kind of alert:** until now only news with a kind read (a contract win, an FDA approval…) had a playbook; everything else was traded the plain way or not at all. Alerts with no kind of news read now get pairs by what raised them: **options flow**, **confluence**, **price moves**, and **other news** (headlines no kind fits). Each gets the whole machinery: every way in paired with every way out, checked out of sample, the pass rule, the 80% range, the 5-minutes-late grade. The playbook autopilot, the playbook today board and the morning game plan use them like any other kind.
- **The playbook reads the stock:** each stock's own record (its personality, from 6+ graded alerts) now changes what the playbook does with it:
  - a stock that **fights its news** (most of its alerts went the other way by the close) is sat out: the autopilot passes, the board says why (*"this stock fights its news here (22% of its 9 alerts went their way)"*), and you vs the playbook counts it as 0%;
  - on a stock whose moves **fade by the close**, the playbook takes the first hour when it entered at the alert or on volume, and otherwise doesn't hold past the close.
- **The playbook coaches your open calls:** the exit rules the playbook learned ran for the paper autopilot only. Your own paper calls from alerts now get them too, as items in **Needs you**, each with a one-tap close:
  - **its way out came:** the playbook's way out for its kind is due (an hour in, at the close, the next day, five sessions on);
  - **stalled:** 30 minutes in it's gone nowhere, and stalled alerts like it lost the rest of the day here;
  - **gave back its run:** in the last 15 minutes before an overnight hold, it gave back the kind of run that lost overnight here;
  - **denied:** a later headline denied or walked back its news.

  Each comes as one push too (Settings → *Coach my calls*). They're the playbook's record, not an order: closing is always your tap.
- **The promise monitor:** every alert goes out stamped with its kind's pair and what that pair had made on average. Once the alert is graded, the desk knows what the pair actually made on an alert it was never fitted on. An alert whose way in never came counts as 0%. Pooled per kind, the newest 15 are the playbook's live record. Below the promised range's bottom (or a point under the promise, without a range) and losing, the kind is **drifting**: the market may have changed under the record. The playbook then sits it out (the autopilot passes, the board says why) until its live alerts recover. The Watch tab's **The playbook's promises, kept?** board shows promised against live per kind. It runs on every stamped alert, whether or not anyone follows the playbook. `GET /api/promises`.
- **Scaling out, graded:** half an hour in and half at the close; half at the close and half the next day; half an hour in and half the next day. Each is a pair like any other, from each half's own entry to its own exit, checked, ranged and passed the same way. A split averages between its two legs, so it's never the best by average; what it buys is steadiness. When one keeps 80%+ of the best pair's average with at least a quarter less swing (standard deviation), it's the **smoother way**: *"…; smoother: half an hour in, half the next day, +2.2% with 50% less swing"*. The playbook takes it:
  - the autopilot opens two half-size paper calls with their own exits (one alert against its daily limit);
  - the playbook today board follows both halves (*"half out"*);
  - the promise is the split's own average.
- **Show the work:** every pair on *The whole playbook, together* board is tappable. It opens the alerts behind its number, newest first (up to 60): the stock, the date, the headline, that pair's result and the plain way's. Tap one to open its sheet. `GET /api/combos/work?kind=…&pair=…`.
- **Event risk on overnight holds:** the playbook's overnight averages were earned on ordinary nights. A night the stock reports earnings (after today's close, or before or on the next session) or has a scheduled binary event (an FDA date, a readout) is a coin toss of a different size, whatever the news was. Every alert now notes whether the night after its session is an **event night** for its stock (Nasdaq's earnings calendar and the desk's captured binary events). Those holds are graded separately. Until 8+ are graded and they paid on average, the playbook doesn't hold through one:
  - the playbook autopilot exits such calls at 15:50 ET, with the reason;
  - the playbook today board flags them before the bell (*"📅✗ ACME reports earnings after the close: the playbook doesn't hold through one until its record says it pays. Out at the close instead."*);
  - *Coach my calls* says the same about yours.

  That's a stated policy until the record has its say, not a finding. The track record panel shows event nights against ordinary ones as they accrue.
- **The playbook's track record:** every alert goes out with what the playbook would do with it: its pair, or a pass. Once graded, the alert says what that did, on an alert the pair was never fitted on, and what the plain way did on the same alert. Added up in order, one equal-sized paper call per alert, they make two lines on the Watch tab's **The playbook's track record**:
  - the playbook's way (a pass or an entry that never came adds 0%);
  - the plain way (in at the alert, out at the close).

  With them come the totals, the average per alert, how often each went its way, how many it took, and the playbook's worst drawdown: *"Since 2026-09-20, on 214 alerts: the playbook +38.1% in all (+0.18% an alert), the plain way +12.4% (+0.06%); its worst drawdown -6.2%."* It runs whether or not anyone follows the playbook. `GET /api/playbook-curve`.
- **The playbook in the pre-trade check:** just before you track a call from an alert, the check now adds the playbook's view, at the moment of decision:
  - *"The playbook passes on this one: …"* (red), with its reason: the kind doesn't pay here, the stock fights its news, the kind is drifting, it's a thin stock;
  - *"The playbook waits: on the second leg, out at the close (+1.4% on average, 20); in now and out at the close made +0.6%"* (caution);
  - *"The playbook takes it, at the alert, out the next day (+1.4% on average, 20): mind the exit"* (good);
  - against the alert's own direction, it says the playbook only trades news its own way.
- **Liquidity bands:** a paper call fills at the alert price. In a stock trading $50M a day, that's roughly what a person gets; in one trading $500K, it isn't. Each alert now carries its stock's dollar volume when it went out: **thin** (under $2M a day), **light** ($2M-20M), **liquid** ($20M-200M) or **deep** ($200M+). Per band, the record grades:
  - the plain way;
  - the same alerts **5 minutes late** (the tradeability test);
  - the playbook's own way.

  When thin stocks lost even 5 minutes late on 15+ graded, their paper edge is a fill nobody gets. The playbook then sits thin stocks out: the autopilot passes, the sheet and the board say why. The Watch tab's **Liquidity** board shows all four bands. `GET /api/liquidity`.
- **The playbook's blind spots:** a playbook picked per kind of news can still lose in places the kind doesn't describe: late-day alerts, Friday news, shorts, small caps, MEDIUM alerts. Every hour its track record is sliced by time of day, day of the week, long or short, company size and severity. A slice is a **blind spot** when the playbook lost there on 20+ alerts, lost in both the older and the newer half of them (one bad stretch doesn't make a rule), and makes money overall. The playbook sits blind spots out: the autopilot passes, and the board and the pre-trade check say why (*"a blind spot of the playbook (time of day: the last hour: -1.00% an alert on 30, losing in both halves of its record)"*). Slices are judged on what the playbook *would* have done, so sitting one out never makes it look recovered. A slice that genuinely recovers drops off on its own. The Watch tab lists them. `GET /api/blindspots`.
- **The tilt guard:** after two or three losers in a row, traders tend to press to win it back. Whether you do is in your own record. Each paper call you opened yourself is sorted by how many losers in a row you'd closed that day before opening it: none, one, two or more (Desk → *After a losing streak*). When your calls after 2+ losses lost on average (5+) and did worse than your calls with none, that's your tilt. While you're on a 2+ streak today, the pre-trade check turns red the moment you go to open another: *"You've closed 2 losers in a row today; your calls opened after 2+ losses the same day made -0.8% (6), against +0.5% otherwise."* Without that record it's a caution. It never blocks the call. `GET /api/tilt` (per phone).
- **Blind spots by market conditions:** some losses are the market's, not the news'. The blind-spot scan adds three slices of the market's own state:
  - **the regime** when the alert went out (risk-on, risk-off, mixed; now stamped on each alert);
  - **today's tape** (cold, normal, hot), for alerts after it was known that day (the first eight alerts' hour), so nothing reads the future;
  - **macro days** (a Fed or jobs day, or an ordinary one).

  The same rule applies: 20+ alerts, losing in both halves of the record, while the playbook makes money overall. A blind spot here might read *"The market: risk-off"*: the playbook sits risk-off days out until they recover.
- **The playbook in dollars:** the track record as an account. Your account size (Settings → account, else $10,000), a tenth of the balance in each paper call, compounded, for the playbook and the plain way: *"$10,000 → $13,812 on the playbook (the plain way $11,240); best month +$1,420, worst -$380; its deepest drawdown -$610."* The last six months are listed too. Paper only.
- **Encryption:** every alert is encrypted on the server for each phone (RFC 8291, "aes128gcm"). Apple and Google carry it but can't read it.
- **Signing:** each push is signed with the server's VAPID key (RFC 8292), so push services only accept pushes from your server.
- **The app shell is public; its data isn't.** `/app/` is only HTML, icons and the service worker. Alerts, settings and subscriptions all need your `DASHBOARD_TOKEN`. The token is stored on the phone so the installed app opens straight in.
- **Your desk** is stored under a random per-phone id (no name, number or email). Anyone with your access key could read a desk if they knew its id; the id is only kept on that phone and in the server's database.
- **Cleanup:** when a phone uninstalls the app or revokes permission, its push service answers *gone* and News247 deletes that subscription.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|
| No **Turn on alerts** button on iPhone | You opened it in Safari, not from the home screen. Add it to the home screen (steps above) and open it from there. iOS must be 16.4 or newer. |
| Control tab shows **Notifications are blocked** | iPhone: Settings → Notifications → Foretape → Allow. Android/desktop: the site's settings in the browser. Then reopen the app and tap **Reconnect**. |
| Test works, but no alerts come later | Open the alert and tap **Did this reach my phone?**: it names what stopped it and fixes it in one tap. **Is it working?** (Control tab or ⌘K) checks the whole path; the **push log** shows what was sent. On Render Free without `STATE_DB`, open Foretape once after a redeploy. |
| "That key didn't work" | Your `DASHBOARD_TOKEN` changed. Open `/app/?token=<new token>` once. If the phone stays subscribed to an old key, tap **Reconnect**. |
| Phones subscribed: 0 after a redeploy | Expected without `STATE_DB`: open Foretape on the phone and it re-registers. |

## How it was verified

The tests (`tests/test_webpush.py`) check:
- the encryption against the official RFC 8291 test vector, byte for byte;
- the VAPID signatures, as a push service would verify them;
- a full alert → encrypted push → decrypted on a simulated phone;
- dead-subscription cleanup and restoring subscriptions after a restart;
- the public/private split of the web routes.

An end-to-end browser run in real Chromium covered:
- tapping **Turn on alerts**;
- the engine publishing a CRITICAL alert, encrypted and signed, through a local push service;
- the service worker showing the notification, with the right title, body, "stay on screen" flag and **Open source** action.

From publish to push took about 3 ms.
