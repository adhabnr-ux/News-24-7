**Question:** for every big move on Monday, did Foretape have it before the price, and if not,
why?

**Data:**
- **Moves:** TradingView's screener (US-traded, $2B+, ≥10% on the day, 500K+ shares), with
  5-minute bars to time each move.
- **Headlines:** TradingView's news feed, with publication timestamps. Story text was read where
  the direction or the subject was unclear (Cerebras).
- **Scoring:** the same replay as the week of Oct 5 (`tests/replaying.py`).
  - Each headline is scored at its publication time plus the time News247 takes to read that

  - Then the scorecard (`news247/scorecard.py`) judges each move.
- **Excluded:** headlines from sources News247 does not read (Zacks, GuruFocus, Binance News).

## Before: the rules as they were on Oct 12

**Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac were invisible.**
1. **Not in the universe.** Both trade over the counter, and Nasdaq's screener (the universe)
   lists only exchange-listed stocks. So the radar never priced them, and the scorecard had no
   move to judge.
2. **Mis-tagged.** "Fannie Mae", "Freddie Mac" and "Fannie and Freddie" were aliases of the
   FHFA (their regulator), whose exposure is FICO, RKT and UWMC. Every headline about them
   tagged those three instead.
3. **Three more scoring gaps:**
   - **Benzinga's "Worthless" story** scored 34 (LOW). Its "Trump-Era Gains" counted as a White
     House action (+12, SPY/QQQ).
   - **Mizuho's downgrade** scored 0. "Cut to Neutral" was not read as a rating change, and
     "release date" was penalized as if it were an earnings-date notice.
   - **Burry's swap into the preferred** scored 15. Nothing knew who Burry is.
4. **Source gap.** Seeking Alpha and Stocktwits aren't read; only Benzinga (Alpaca) was.

FICO, CBRS and SMTC were judged as they are below; those rules didn't change.

## After: what changed

- **Universe:** `OTC_LISTINGS` (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) is merged into every Nasdaq refresh and
  every cached universe. The sweep prices them from Yahoo every few minutes, and their names
  are recognized in headlines.
- **Knowledge:**
  - **Fannie Mae (FNMA)** and **Freddie Mac (FMCC)** are companies of their own.
  - The FHFA keeps its own names and now exposes FNMA and FMCC first.
  - **Michael Burry** is a known name.
- **Rules:**
  - `gse_release`: conservatorship, release, IPO, preferred, warrants, dilution. Tags FNMA and
    FMCC.
  - `star_investor_bet`: Burry, Chanos and Einhorn warning, shorting, dumping or swapping. Its
    direction is down.
  - Seeking Alpha's rating-change wording ("cut to Neutral", "lowered to Underperform", "raised
    to Buy") counts as a downgrade or upgrade, and sets the direction.
  - "Worthless" is a keyword and a down word.
  - The earnings "release date" penalty now needs earnings-date wording ("release date for",
    "sets … release date", "announces … release date").
  - A name used as an era ("Trump-era", "Biden-era") is no longer read as that person acting.
- **Source:** `gnews-gse-investors`, a Google News search for Fannie, Freddie, FHFA, Pulte,
  Burry, Chanos and Einhorn, polled every 90 s. In the replay it is given 30 minutes, for the
  time Google takes to index a story.
- **The play:** the stock that the closest precedent moved leads the play. "@pulte: Fannie Mae
  and Freddie Mac will accept VantageScore…" names the GSEs, but FICO crashed both times, so FICO
  leads.
- **The options tape** skips the OTC names, which have no listed options.
- **Found on the way:**
  - The overnight memory (news kept up to four days for the next session's alerts) was pruned
    against the wall clock, so a replayed day older than four days lost its own overnight news.
  - It is now counted from each item's detection time, which is the same thing live.
  - The CCI replay test had started failing on Oct 13 for this reason alone.

## What the replay can't tell you

The replay uses today's rules on Monday's headlines. The live system ran the old rules on Monday,
so its scorecard (`news247 scorecard --date 2026-10-12`) should:
- have **no FNMA or FMCC rows** at all, because the radar never saw them;
- show FICO, CBRS and SMTC with whatever the live feeds actually read. For example, the WSJ
  story only counts if a feed News247 read on Monday carried it.

Comparing the two is how a gap that the replay misses gets found.
